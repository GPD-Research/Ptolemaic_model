# Chapter PT-5: The Precession of the Equinoxes

Hipparchus, comparing his star positions with those of Timocharis about 150 years earlier, found that the stars' longitudes had all grown: the whole sphere of the fixed stars turns slowly eastward about the poles of the ecliptic, while the equinoxes stay put. Ptolemy confirmed it (Almagest VII.2-3) and fixed the rate at **1° per 100 years**.

## Ptolemy's rate and the real one
The real rate is about 50.3″ a year, **1° per 72 years**, one full turn in about 25,800 years. Ptolemy's rate is too slow by about a third. He also used it to bring Hipparchus's positions to his own epoch: 265 years × 1°/century gives the 2°40′ he added, where the real shift was about 3°40′. So his catalogue starts about 1° behind the real sky of AD 137, and the gap grows with time: about 8–9° today.

## What it means today
Because the zodiac signs are counted from the spring equinox while the constellations drift, the sign Aries no longer contains the stars of Aries: the equinox has moved into Pisces. Precession also moves the celestial pole: Polaris was not near the pole in Ptolemy's day. The cause, the Moon's and Sun's pull on Earth's equatorial bulge making its axis wobble like a top, had to wait for Newton. `src/precession.py` gives both rates; the viewer's 3-D sphere shows the drift.
