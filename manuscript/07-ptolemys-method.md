# Chapter PT-7: Ptolemy's Method

Ptolemy's cosmology rests on wrong premises: a stationary central Earth and motion built only from circles. His *method*, though, is recognisably scientific, and it is why the *Almagest* lasted fourteen centuries.

* **Observations first.** Each model is derived from dated observations, many listed with time and place, his own and those of Hipparchus and the Babylonians.
* **Explicit models with few parameters.** Each body gets a small set of numbers (eccentricity, epicycle radius, apogee, mean motions, epoch values) fixed from a minimum of chosen observations, for example three oppositions for each outer planet.
* **Prediction and checking.** The fitted model is checked against other observations, and the *Almagest* gives tables from which anyone could compute positions for any date and test them.
* **Publishing everything.** Parameters, derivations and tables are all given, so later astronomers (al-Battani, Ibn al-Shatir, Copernicus) could test and correct them.
* **Simplicity as a principle.** He prefers the simplest hypothesis that saves the phenomena, but adds a device, such as the equant, when observations demand it.

Where it went wrong is instructive too: some of his "observations" look adjusted to fit his theory (his equinox times and star longitudes), and the models fit angles while failing on distances (Chapter PT-3 and the README's experiments). Copernicus kept most of the method and changed the premise.
